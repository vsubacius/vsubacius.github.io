Air path decisions

Pump pulls from behind the sensors
The pump's inlet connects after the chamber, so its heat, motor smell and pulses stay out of the sample. Exhaust leaves at the rear, far from the nostrils.
Two nostrils, one job each
The sample side has only a dust filter. The clean-air side holds about 1.8 g of activated carbon between two particle filters. It screws out for replacement.
Servo turns a 3-way valve
A sliding shutter would connect the two nostrils and leak room air, so the servo turns a sealed Luer stopcock instead. It holds position with the servo unpowered and adds a third state that seals the chamber.
A small restriction sets the flow
A 25 G dispensing tip drops about 13 kPa at 300 mL/min, while the carbon bed drops about 6 Pa. The tip dominates, so both nostrils draw nearly the same flow and the sensors see no cooling step when the valve moves.
Buffer smooths the pulses
A 13 mL volume between the tip and the pump acts as a pneumatic low-pass filter, with a corner near 0.5 Hz, well below the pump's pulse rate.
Only the Figaro cans enter the chamber
Each module sits upside down on the chamber lid with its can sealed through a hole. The trimmer, comparator and flux stay outside the sample.
BME690s come first
Hot MOX cans warm the air and burn off a little of the odor, so the BME690s read it upstream of them.
Pump runs from the battery
The datasheet use range is 3.0–4.5 V, so a 5 V rail is outside it. Battery power also keeps motor noise off the 5 V heater rail.
