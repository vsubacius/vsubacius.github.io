Air path decisions

Straight sample path
The sample nostril, the valve and the chamber inlet are on one line. The medical stopcock has no upstream tube. The Hamilton valve has one short PFA tube.
Pump pulls from behind the sensors
The pump inlet is after the chamber, the restrictor and the buffer. Its heat, motor smell and pulses stay out of the sample. The exhaust goes out at the rear.
One-piece aluminum chamber
The block is open at the bottom and seals to the hub with one FKM gasket. The shuttle is fully inside. There is no lid joint.
BME690s come first
Hot MOX cans warm the air and use some of the odor, so the BME690s read the air upstream of the cans.
Only the Figaro cans enter the chamber
Each module is upside down on the chamber top. An FKM O-ring seals each can body. The trim pot, comparator and flux stay outside.
A small restriction sets the flow
A 25 G tip drops about 13 kPa at 300 mL/min. The carbon bed drops about 6 Pa. So the two nostrils draw almost the same flow.
Low pressure only after the tip
The chamber is near room pressure during flow. Only the buffer and the pump tube are at about −13 kPa, and their joints are threads, gaskets and clamped silicone.
Wetted materials
Aluminum 6061, FKM, PFA and PTFE tape, plus parts that cannot change: the stopcock, the polypropylene liners, the shuttle board, the cans, the mesh and the membrane. No silicone before the buffer.
